Earth map data sources

Building footprints and available heights come from © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), in the © OpenMapTiles schema, served by OpenFreeMap. Buildings without measured heights use the source's estimated height, or a 6 m fallback. Footprint buildings use the available roof shape and height tags or a flat roof. Facade windows and their illumination are illustrative.

Satellite imagery and elevation: © Mapbox, with the underlying providers credited by Mapbox. Optional U.S. high-resolution terrain packs contain public-domain USGS 3D Elevation Program bare-earth elevation. Each pack records its source surveys in sources.json.

Night-light emission uses the NASA Black Marble 2016 grayscale composite, from Suomi NPP VIIRS data. NASA Earth Observatory / Joshua Stevens; data from Miguel Román, NASA Goddard Space Flight Center. The 3 km image was downsampled to 8192 × 4096 for this globe. This is a historical composite, not a live view of power usage.

Cloud coverage uses NASA's Blue Marble (2002) cloud composite. Image: Reto Stöckli, NASA Goddard Space Flight Center; enhancements by Robert Simmon. The 2048 × 1024 image is encoded as a grayscale coverage texture. The historical coverage field seeds procedural cloud volumes for the ten main WMO cloud families. The optional NOAA Global Forecast System supplies 1-degree low, middle, and high cloud cover for the UTC forecast time shown in the controls. Individual cloud shapes, altitude, wind, and lighting are simulated.

Cloud rendering references: Guerrilla Games: Nubis; Epic Games: Volumetric Clouds; WMO International Cloud Atlas. Procedural noise and shaders are locally authored approximations.

The optional ocean-floor layer shows shaded depths from the NOAA NCEI ETOPO 2022 global relief model, released under CC0.

The building decoder uses vector-tile-js, pbf, and earcut; open-source licenses.

Star positions, magnitudes and colors come from Hoffleit and Warren (1991), Yale Bright Star Catalogue, 5th revised edition, distributed by NASA HEASARC. This view includes stars brighter than magnitude 5.8.

The optional open elevation layer uses © Mapterhorn and its source survey providers. The PMTiles reader and its decompressor use BSD and MIT licenses. Regional mountain shadows and backup land elevations use the 60 arc-second ice-surface grid of ETOPO 2022, NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information. Static high-resolution US sample terrain comes from the USGS 3D Elevation Program.

Tree heights use the 2020 global 10 m canopy model by Lang, Jetz, Schindler and Wegner (CC BY 4.0), served by Esri. Regional forest types use the 2020 NASA MODIS IGBP classification, distributed through GIBS. Individual positions and tree forms are illustrative. Crown images are derived from CC0 Poly Haven tree models. The Esri LERC decoder is licensed under Apache 2.0.

Optional city surveys include the Helsinki 3D city model, City of Helsinki 2024 and © 3DBAG by tudelft3d and 3DGI, both licensed under CC BY 4.0. The renderer converts coordinates, selects levels of detail, adjusts texture resolution and shades the models for this globe. Textured Japanese buildings come from Project PLATEAU, Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, Japan. The nationwide Luxembourg photomesh uses summer 2020 vertical and oblique imagery, released under CC0 by the Administration du cadastre et de la topographie. Swiss building models are © swisstopo. Broad coverage boundaries use public-domain Natural Earth data. Meshes stream from the original providers. Vertical datum conversion uses NGA EGM96. Emissive windows on surveyed buildings are simulated and respond to regional night radiance.

The optional close-range US surface uses classified airborne ground, water and road returns from the public-domain USGS 3D Elevation Program and its public Entwine Point Tiles archive. Heights follow the survey acquisition date. The native terrain shader supplies the chosen map style. Consistent airborne returns within low buildings with nominal heights can refine those extrusions. LAZ decoding uses laz-perf (Apache 2.0). Textured city models use Google Draco (Apache 2.0) and NASA-AMMOS 3D Tiles Renderer (Apache 2.0).

Mapped water boundaries use the water polygons in the same OpenFreeMap / OpenMapTiles vector tiles, © OpenStreetMap contributors. Water reflections, waves and atmospheric lighting are simulated. The optional mapped street-light layer loads positions from the Overture Maps Foundation infrastructure dataset, derived from © OpenStreetMap contributors under the Open Database License. Download the derived lamp-location database. Lamp intensity, fixture height and operating hours are modeled.

Lunar surface reflectance uses the NASA Scientific Visualization Studio CGI Moon Kit, derived from Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera imagery. Lunar orientation follows the NASA NAIF planetary constants kernel.
